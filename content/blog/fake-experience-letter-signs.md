Resume fraud is common enough that most HR teams have met it. Fake experience letters, edited relieving letters and padded tenures are among the easiest ways to improve a profile, and among the hardest to spot by eye. Industry reports on background checks consistently find that employment history is among the areas where discrepancies appear most often.

## Signs a letter may be fake or edited

- **Format problems:** inconsistent fonts, spacing or alignment, or a logo that looks stretched or low quality.
- **Dates that do not add up:** the joining date before the company existed, overlapping jobs, or a gap that matches nothing.
- **Generic wording:** no designation details, no specific role, only praise.
- **Odd signatories:** a name with no title, or a person who left before the date of the letter.
- **Contact details that do not match** the company's real website or number.
- **A personal email** address for the "HR" contact.
- **Unusual urgency:** pressure to accept the letter without checking.

## Why comparing papers is not enough

A good forger can copy a template well. Editing tools make it easy to alter dates and titles. If you only compare papers, you are checking the forger's skill, not the facts.

## Checks that work better

1. **Call the employer's HR yourself.** Find the number on the company's official website, not from the letter.
2. **Ask specific questions:** dates, designation, reason for leaving, eligibility for rehire.
3. **Use independent records with consent.** EPFO history, where the person has a UAN, can show employers and dates.
4. **Compare with other documents:** payslips, bank statements and tax forms should tell the same story.
5. **Ask the candidate to explain.** Let them talk through their role and projects. People who really did the job can describe it.
6. **Treat mismatches as questions.** There are many honest causes, such as company renaming or a delay in records.

## What to do when you find a fake

Document what you found, give the candidate a fair chance to explain, and follow your company's policy. Avoid public accusations.
