People who cook, drive, garden or help at home are part of your daily life, and you give them access to your family, your things and your routines. A short, standard verification before they start protects everyone, including the worker.

## Why one checklist

Different roles carry different risks, but the basics are the same: know who they are, where they live and who can vouch for them. A single checklist you apply to everyone is fair and easy to follow.

## The checklist for every helper

1. **Identity:** a government photo ID, and a recent photograph.
2. **Address:** current address and a permanent address, with a contact in the family.
3. **Contact numbers:** their own and a relative's.
4. **References:** previous employers, contacted by you.
5. **Police verification** as per local rules.
6. **Health:** basic fitness and any condition relevant to the job, such as food handling.
7. **Written terms:** hours, pay, leave and duties.

## Add by role

- **Cook:** hygiene practice, and food-allergy awareness in your family.
- **Driver:** a valid licence for your vehicle type, a trial drive, and a record of challans where available.
- **Gardener or maintenance:** tools they bring, and whether they work alone.
- **Caregiver for children or elders:** more references, a trial period and a check for any history of concern.

## Tips for a respectful process

- Explain why you ask, and tell them how their data will be used
- Do not hold on to original documents
- Keep copies safely and delete them when they leave
- Meet the worker's agency, if any, and check its own verification

## When something feels wrong

If a helper avoids sharing ID, gives details that do not match or has no references, pause and ask calmly. A trial period of a few weeks, with clear tasks and regular feedback, helps you build trust. Always let family members know who is coming into the home and when.
