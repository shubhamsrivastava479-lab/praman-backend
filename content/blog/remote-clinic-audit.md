Insurers and TPAs rely on clinics and diagnostic centres to treat and test their members. Most are honest. A few inflate bills, bill for tests never done or exist only on paper. Auditing every site in person is slow and costly, which makes remote audits attractive.

## Why it matters

Claims from a single doubtful clinic can be large, and repeated. Early checks protect honest members and keep premiums fair.

## What a remote audit looks at

1. **Existence and address:** the premises match the registered address, with a clear signboard.
2. **Registration and licences:** display of valid registration and staff qualifications.
3. **Equipment:** scan, lab and other machines that are billed for are present and working.
4. **Staff:** doctors and technicians who are named in records are actually on site.
5. **Patient load:** a reasonable number of patients for the claims being raised.
6. **Records:** registers, reports and billing match.
7. **Hygiene and safety basics.**

## How to run it on video

- Schedule the call in clinic hours
- Ask the owner or manager to walk with the camera
- Record location and time
- Ask for random items, such as today's patient register or equipment serial numbers
- Compare what you see with the claims raised

## Signs to follow up

- The site cannot be shown live or the camera keeps moving away
- Machines named in bills are not on site
- Staff cannot explain procedures
- The address is a residence or shared space

## Report what you saw

A good audit report lists what was seen, what was not shown and what needs follow-up, with the date, time and person who joined. Share findings with the clinic and give it a fair chance to explain before any action is taken.
