A new supplier, distributor or service vendor gets access to your money, your goods and sometimes your customers. Most are genuine. But fake firms, shell companies and changed bank accounts are among the most common business frauds. A standard checklist at onboarding catches most problems before the first payment.

## Why vendor verification matters

- **Fake or shell firms** that disappear after an advance.
- **GST mismatch** that can affect your input tax credit.
- **Bank account fraud:** payments sent to an account not in the vendor's name.
- **Address that does not exist** or is only a mailing address.
- **Conflicts of interest:** a vendor secretly linked to an employee.

## The checklist

1. **Registration:** company or LLP details from the official registry; partnership or proprietorship proof.
2. **GST:** GSTIN active, legal name and address matching.
3. **PAN** of the business and the owners or directors.
4. **Bank account:** the account holder's name matches the vendor.
5. **Address:** the office, shop or factory actually exists and operates.
6. **Owners and directors:** identity, and any court cases.
7. **Capacity:** staff, machinery, stock, compared with what they promise.
8. **References** from existing customers.

## Do not skip the site check

Documents can be correct while the business behind them is not. A live look at the premises (people working, stock, signboard, machinery) tells you more than any certificate.

## Warning signs during onboarding

- The vendor pushes for a large advance before any delivery.
- The GST legal name and the bank account name are different.
- The "office" is a co-working desk or a residential flat with no sign of business.
- Bank details change by email soon after onboarding. Always confirm a change by calling a number you already have.
- The vendor was introduced by a single employee who also approves their bills.
