A second-hand car at a fair price can turn into an expensive one if it comes with unpaid traffic fines, a loan still in place or papers that do not match. Pending e-challans are one of the easiest things to check and one of the most often skipped.

## Why e-challans matter

Challans are issued against the vehicle's registration. Cameras and traffic systems send them automatically, and many owners find out only later. Unpaid challans can lead to notices, court summons in some cases, and delays at transfer or renewal.

## What to check before buying

1. **Registration certificate (RC):** owner name, chassis and engine numbers, and registration date.
2. **Pending e-challans:** all outstanding fines, with dates and places.
3. **Loan or hypothecation:** whether a bank still has a claim.
4. **Insurance:** validity and claims history.
5. **Fitness and pollution certificates** where required.
6. **Seller identity:** ID and a face match, and whether the seller is the registered owner.
7. **Physical inspection:** chassis number match, and signs of accident or repair.

## How to check challans

- Search the registration number on the official parivahan or state traffic portal
- Note the amount and date of each challan
- Ask the seller to pay them and share the receipt
- Recheck just before transfer

## How to protect yourself

- Do not pay the full amount until challans are cleared and papers are ready
- Put the clearing of challans in the written agreement
- Complete transfer promptly after purchase
- Keep all receipts and screenshots

## Also worth knowing

Some challans are disputed or already paid but not yet updated in the system. Ask the seller for receipts, and keep your own screenshot of the portal on the day you check. If a large amount is pending, negotiate the price or ask the seller to clear it before the deal is final.
