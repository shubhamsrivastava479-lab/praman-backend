When a used car changes hands, people focus on the price, the RC and the insurance. The FASTag often gets forgotten. Then toll charges hit the old owner's wallet, the new owner is stopped at a toll plaza, or a blacklisted tag causes delays. A few minutes at the time of sale avoids all this.

## Why it matters

A FASTag is linked to a vehicle number and to the owner's KYC and wallet. When ownership changes, both need updating, or there may be a mismatch between the vehicle's owner and the tag's owner.

## What the seller should do

1. **Check the balance** and any pending charges.
2. **Close or deregister the tag** with the issuer once the vehicle is sold, and take any balance returned.
3. **Remove the tag** from the windshield if the issuer asks, so it is not reused.
4. **Share proof** of closure with the buyer.

## What the buyer should check

1. **Is there a tag on the windshield,** and is it active?
2. **Whose name is it in?** If it is the seller's, it should be closed and a new one taken.
3. **Is there a pending toll dispute or a blacklist status?**
4. **Take a new tag** in your name with your KYC and the updated RC.
5. **Update the RC first,** as many issuers need it.

## Check the rest at the same time

- Pending e-challans on the registration number
- Hypothecation or loan on the RC
- Insurance transfer and no-claim bonus
- Owner identity, matching ID and RC

## Common mistakes

- Leaving the old tag active, so tolls keep debiting the seller
- Paying the full price before the transfer is done
- Not keeping receipts and screenshots

## Keep proof

Save a screenshot or message showing that the old tag is closed and the new one is active, along with the date. If a toll dispute arises later, these simple records settle it quickly.
