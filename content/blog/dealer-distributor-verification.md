A strong dealer and distributor network is how many companies reach customers. It also exposes the company to risks: unpaid credit, grey-market sales, misuse of the brand and partners that are not what they say. A clear verification routine at onboarding keeps the network healthy.

## Why it matters

Dealers receive stock on credit and talk to customers in your name. Problems often show up only after money is lost. Fixing a bad onboarding is far costlier than a few extra checks at the start.

## The checklist

1. **Who they are:** the owner or partners' identity, with a live face match.
2. **Business existence:** registration, shop or office address, board and premises.
3. **Registrations:** GST, PAN, trade licence and other documents the business needs.
4. **Bank account:** name matching the business, and a cancelled cheque or statement.
5. **Financial standing:** turnover, other brands, past dues and credit history.
6. **Premises and capacity:** storage, staff and vehicles.
7. **References:** other suppliers and customers, called by you.
8. **Disputes:** known cases or complaints involving the business or its owners.
9. **Agreement:** written terms on territory, pricing, credit and brand use.

## Ongoing monitoring

- Review sales patterns and payment behaviour
- Revisit when ownership or address changes
- Check for sales outside the agreed territory
- Respond quickly to customer complaints

## Red flags

- A newly formed business asking for a large credit limit
- Premises that do not match the claim
- Owner unwilling to meet or show documents
- A bank account in a different name

## Start small, grow with trust

Give new partners a modest credit limit and a short probation, then raise it as payments and sales come in on time. Clear written terms and regular reviews protect both sides and keep honest partners motivated.
