A driving licence is the first document any employer or fleet owner asks a driver for. It is also one of the easiest to fake or borrow. Checking it online takes minutes, and the details it shows can save you from a serious problem.

## What to collect

- The driver's **licence number**
- The driver's **date of birth**
- A clear **photo of the licence**, front and back
- A **live photo or video** of the driver so you can match the face

## Step by step

1. **Check the number format.** Licence numbers follow a pattern with state and RTO codes. A number that does not look right deserves a question.
2. **Verify against the official record.** The government's transport portal, Parivahan, lets you look up licence details. A verification service can also fetch the record for you, with consent, from the same underlying source.
3. **Compare each detail:** name, date of birth, address, photo.
4. **Check validity.** Is the licence active, and when does it expire?
5. **Check the vehicle classes.** Does it cover a light motor vehicle, a heavy goods vehicle or a transport vehicle, whichever the job needs?
6. **Look at endorsements and restrictions** where shown.
7. **Match the face** on the licence with the person in front of you.

## Common signs of a problem

- The name or date of birth does not match what the driver told you
- The licence has expired or does not cover your vehicle type
- The photo does not match
- The licence number cannot be found
- The driver hesitates or asks you not to check

## Go beyond the licence

A licence proves driving permission. For a person who will drive your family or your goods, also check identity, address, previous employers and, for high-trust roles, court records with consent.
