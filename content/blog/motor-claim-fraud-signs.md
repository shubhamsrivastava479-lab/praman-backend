Motor insurance is among the most claimed types of cover. Most claims are honest: a minor accident, a damaged bumper, a stolen vehicle. A few are not, and the cost of those shows up in everyone's premium. Investigators look for patterns that suggest a closer look is needed. Knowing them helps genuine customers prepare and claims teams focus.

## Common types of motor claim fraud

**1. Staged accidents.** A collision arranged to claim for damage or injury.

**2. Pre-existing damage.** Old damage presented as part of a new accident.

**3. Inflated repair bills.** Real damage, but with parts or labour padded, or new parts billed that were never fitted.

**4. False theft claims.** A vehicle reported stolen when it was sold, hidden or abandoned.

**5. Driver substitution.** A different person was driving, often someone without a valid licence, and the named driver is substituted after the accident.

**6. Policy back-dating.** A policy taken after an accident and presented as active at the time.

**7. Fake documents.** Edited bills, forged FIRs or altered RC and licence papers.

## Warning signs investigators look for

- A claim soon after the policy started or renewed after a lapse
- No police report where one would be expected
- Accident location that does not match the story
- Damage that does not fit the described accident
- Statements from the owner, driver and witnesses that differ
- A garage that is unfamiliar or far from the accident site
- Repeated claims by the same person or vehicle
- Delay in reporting without a good reason

## What genuine claimants can do

- Report the accident as soon as possible
- Photograph the vehicle and the scene
- Keep the FIR, estimates and bills
- Give a clear, consistent account
- Be honest about who was driving

## How investigators check

Investigators examine the vehicle and papers, speak to the owner and driver and compare their accounts, and check the location and timeline. Instant checks on the vehicle, such as RC details and pending challans, support the story or raise questions.
