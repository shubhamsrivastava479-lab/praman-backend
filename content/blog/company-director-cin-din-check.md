Before you sign a contract, give an advance or extend credit to a company, you want to know that it exists and who runs it. Two numbers help: the company's CIN and its directors' DINs. Used well, they give a quick view of whether a business is what it claims.

## Why it matters

Shell companies, struck-off entities and lookalike names are used to take advances and disappear. A few minutes of checking the public record can prevent a large loss.

## What the numbers show

- **CIN:** the company's name, type, state, year of incorporation and registration number.
- **Company status:** active, struck off or under another status.
- **Directors:** names and DINs of current and past directors.
- **Registered address:** where it is officially located.
- **Filings:** whether annual returns and financials are filed on time.

## How to check

1. **Ask for the CIN and certificate of incorporation.**
2. **Look up the CIN on the official corporate affairs portal** and compare the name, address and status.
3. **Check the directors** and compare them with the people you are dealing with.
4. **Match other details:** GST, PAN and bank account should show the company's name.
5. **Search for disputes** and news about the company and its directors.
6. **Confirm the person you speak to** is authorised to act for the company.

## Red flags

- A company incorporated only recently, with a large order or loan request
- Directors who appear in many unrelated or struck-off companies
- A registered address that is a residence or an empty office
- The signatory is not a director or authorised person

## Keep a record

Save screenshots of the registry page with the date, and note who you spoke to. If a deal later goes wrong, this record shows what you checked and when. For larger amounts, repeat the check just before payment, because a company's status and directors can change.
