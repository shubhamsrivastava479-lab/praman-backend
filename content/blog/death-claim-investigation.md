A death claim is among the most sensitive a family ever makes. When an insurer says it will investigate, families often fear a denial. In reality, most investigated claims are genuine and paid. The check exists to confirm facts, especially where the claim is early or large.

## Why claims are investigated

- The death happened within the first few years of the policy
- The sum assured is high compared with the person's income
- Medical history was not disclosed at the time of buying
- The cause of death is unclear or involves an accident
- Details in documents do not match

## What investigators check

1. **Identity:** the deceased and the policyholder are the same person.
2. **Cause and circumstances of death:** hospital records, death certificate and, if needed, FIR and post-mortem.
3. **Medical history:** records before the policy was bought.
4. **Proposal details:** answers given on health, habits and income.
5. **Nominee and family:** the nominee is who the policy says, and the family can explain the events.
6. **Premium history:** payments were made as stated.

## How families can prepare

- Keep the policy, death certificate and hospital papers together
- Be honest and clear when answering questions
- Share all documents the first time so there is no back-and-forth
- Ask the insurer for a written list of what is pending

## What a good investigation looks like

It is respectful, time-bound and shows the family what is needed. Speaking to relatives, neighbours and doctors is done gently and with care for grief.

## A note on sensitivity

Grief makes paperwork harder. Good insurers keep questions short, explain why each document is needed and give one point of contact. If you feel the process is unclear, ask for the pending list in writing and keep a note of each call and date.
