Hiring decisions are made on trust: you trust that the resume is true, that the person in the interview is the person who joins, and that nothing serious has been hidden. Background verification (BGV) turns that trust into evidence.

## How common are discrepancies?

More common than most HR teams expect. In AuthBridge's Workforce Fraud Files for the first half of FY26, 4.33% of white-collar checks showed a discrepancy, with **employment history (11.15%)** and **address (7.68%)** the biggest problem areas, followed by education (4.49%) ([The Hans India](https://www.thehansindia.com/amp/business/surge-in-hiring-glitches-hits-companies-report-1049872)). For gig workers, about 1 in 10 address checks did not match.

## The standard checks

1. **Identity:** PAN and Aadhaar confirmed against the person (face match), not just collected.
2. **Address:** current and permanent, by physical visit or digitally with live location.
3. **Employment history:** dates, designation and reason for leaving; EPFO records help confirm employers and dates.
4. **Education:** degree or marksheet with the issuing body.
5. **Court records:** civil and criminal cases.
6. **References:** a former manager or colleague.

## Typical timelines

Identity checks can be instant. Physical address checks often take several days. Employment and education depend on replies from employers and universities. The slowest part is usually not the checking itself but **waiting for the candidate**: collecting documents, scheduling visits, chasing missing papers.

## Where the process breaks

- Candidates drop out when onboarding drags.
- Bulk hiring (security, delivery, retail, warehouses) cannot wait a week per person.
- Proxy interviews and AI-edited documents are harder to catch on paper.
- Records are scattered across emails, so the evidence is hard to show later.
