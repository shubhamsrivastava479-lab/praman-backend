Choosing a verification partner looks like a price comparison, but the real difference shows up when something goes wrong. A good partner gives you reliable evidence, clear reports and honest answers. A weak one gives you a tick-box and a bill.

## Why the choice matters

Your decisions to hire, lend, rent, insure or pay depend on the report. If it is slow, vague or unreliable, you either take risks or lose good candidates and customers.

## What to evaluate

1. **Coverage:** which checks are offered, and which are live today versus planned? Ask for a clear list.
2. **Evidence quality:** do you get recordings, photos, locations, timestamps and source records, or just a status?
3. **Consent and privacy:** is consent taken and recorded, and how long is data kept?
4. **Security:** where is data stored, who can see it, and what happens when you stop using the service?
5. **Turnaround:** typical and worst-case times, and how delays are communicated.
6. **Accuracy and review:** is there human review of flagged cases, and how are errors corrected?
7. **Scale:** can they handle bulk uploads and spikes?
8. **Integration:** is there a simple way to send cases and get results back, such as Excel, WhatsApp or an API?
9. **Pricing:** is it clear, per check, with no hidden extras?
10. **Support:** can you reach a person quickly?

## Questions to ask

- What can you not verify, and how do you show that in the report?
- Can I see a sample report?
- What happens if a check fails or the source is down?
- Who reviews the result before I get it?

## Red flags in a provider

- Promises of instant results for every check without explaining sources
- No sample report or no way to see evidence
- Vague answers about where data is stored
- No human review of flagged results

A partner that is open about limits is usually more reliable than one that claims to do everything.
