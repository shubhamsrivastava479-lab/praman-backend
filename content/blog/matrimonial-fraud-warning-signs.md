Most matrimonial matches are honest. But a small number of people use matrimonial sites and family networks to cheat. Knowing the common patterns can save a family money, time and heartbreak.

## The common types

**1. Fake or borrowed profile.** The photos belong to someone else, or the person's job, education or family is invented.

**2. The "money emergency".** After a few chats, the match claims an urgent problem: a customs fee for a gift, a medical bill, a business loss. They ask for money. Once it is sent, they disappear.

**3. The "investment" angle.** A match overseas or far away suggests a joint investment. The goal is to move your money.

**4. Hidden previous marriage or children.** Details that are not disclosed until after commitments are made.

**5. Inflated job and income.** A job title, employer or salary that does not exist or is greatly exaggerated.

## Warning signs

- Refuses a video call or always has an excuse for a poor connection
- Moves the chat quickly to private messaging and pushes for secrecy
- Talks about money, gifts or investments early
- Story details change between conversations
- Does not want families to meet, or avoids giving a home address
- Pressure to decide quickly

## A calm way to verify

1. **See the person live** on a video call before you trust the profile.
2. **Ask for consent-based verification** of identity, job and address, and offer the same from your side.
3. **Check details independently:** call the employer's main number, not a number the match gives you.
4. **Meet with family**, in a public place or at home, before any financial talk.
5. **Never send money** before the wedding, whatever the reason.
