In insurance, 'login' means a proposal has been submitted to the insurer. The next step is often a post-login verification, or PLVC. It is a quick check that the information in the proposal is correct and that the customer agrees to it. It is a short step, but it prevents many problems later.

## Why it matters

Errors and misstatements in a proposal, such as a wrong date of birth, an unreported illness or a nominee detail, can lead to claim disputes. Catching them at login stage is easier and kinder than finding them at claim time.

## What PLVC covers

1. **Identity:** the person confirms their name and date of birth.
2. **Contact and address:** current details for communication.
3. **Plan and premium:** what was chosen, how much and how often.
4. **Proposal details:** income, occupation and other answers.
5. **Health and habits** where relevant.
6. **Nominee:** name and relationship.
7. **Consent:** the customer confirms that they filled in or approved the proposal.
8. **Documents received:** whether the customer has the required papers.

## PLVC and PIVC

The names are used differently by different insurers. In general, PIVC emphasises that the customer understood the product and was not mis-sold. PLVC emphasises that the proposal data is correct. Many insurers combine the two in one call.

## Good practice

- Call at a time convenient for the customer
- Use their language
- Ask open questions
- Record the answers and flag mismatches before issuance
- Let the customer correct information

## Tips for customers

Keep your proposal copy and documents ready. Answer honestly. If something looks wrong in the proposal, say so on the call.

## Why it helps everyone

A short, clear call at this stage means fewer corrections, faster issuance and fewer disputes. Customers know exactly what they signed up for, and insurers know the record matches the facts.
