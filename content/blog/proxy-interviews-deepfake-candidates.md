Remote interviews opened up hiring, but they also opened a door to impersonation. Employers now see candidates who are coached off-camera, interviews taken by a skilled friend, and in some cases altered faces or voices. The person who clears the interview is not always the one who turns up on day one.

## Why it matters

A hire who cannot do the job, or who is not who they claimed, costs months of salary, project delays and sometimes access to sensitive systems and data. The harm grows with the level of access the role has.

## How the tricks work

- **Proxy candidate:** a skilled person takes the interview in place of the real candidate.
- **Off-camera help:** someone feeds answers through a second screen or earpiece.
- **Edited video or voice:** tools change the face or voice in real time.
- **Fake documents:** edited degrees and experience letters to match the story.
- **Different person on joining:** the person who joins is not the one who was interviewed.

## Warning signs

- The candidate looks away often or pauses before every answer
- Audio and lip movement are slightly out of sync
- Refusal to turn the camera on or to move the camera when asked
- Camera quality gets worse or changes on request
- Different voice or style between rounds

## Practical steps

1. **Match the face with the ID** at the start of a round, and use a liveness prompt.
2. **Ask unexpected, practical questions** and screen share to solve small tasks.
3. **Use the same verified person** at each round, and compare.
4. **Verify documents** such as degree and previous employment independently.
5. **Re-verify at joining,** in person or on video, before giving access.
6. **Limit access** during probation for sensitive roles.
