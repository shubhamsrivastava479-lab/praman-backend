Second-hand phones, laptops, bikes, cars and even property listings are bought and sold between strangers every day. Most deals go well. The ones that do not often follow a pattern: a great price, a convincing story, and pressure to pay before you have seen anything.

## Why these deals go wrong

- The item does not exist, or the photos are copied from another listing.
- The seller is not the owner.
- The item has a hidden problem or is stolen.
- The seller takes an advance and disappears.
- The payment goes to an account that is not in the seller's name.

## Checks to do before you pay

1. **Search the photos.** Do they appear in other listings with different details?
2. **Ask for a live video call.** Ask the seller to show the item, its serial or IMEI number, bills and the seller's own face.
3. **Match names.** The name on the ID, the bill or RC and the account receiving money should be the same.
4. **For vehicles:** check the RC details, pending challans and whether any loan is marked against it.
5. **Meet in a safe place** where you can inspect the item, ideally with someone you trust.
6. **Pay safely.** No large advance to strangers. Prefer payment on handover, and pay to an account in the seller's name.
7. **Keep records:** chat, receipts and the video call.

## Warning signs

- The price is far below the market with no reason
- "Urgent sale, leaving the country or city"
- Refusal to show the item live or to meet
- Requests for gift cards, "token money" or payment to a third person's account
- Pushing you to move away from the platform's chat or payment system
