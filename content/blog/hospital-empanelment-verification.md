A cashless network is only as trustworthy as the hospitals in it. When an insurer or TPA empanels a hospital, it is promising policyholders that they will be treated there and promising itself that the bills it receives are real. A careful verification at the start prevents many disputes later.

## Why empanelment checks matter

- **Existence:** some "hospitals" are little more than a signboard and a few beds.
- **Capacity claims:** the number of beds, ICU facilities and specialists may be overstated.
- **Registration:** a missing or expired registration is a basic red flag.
- **Billing behaviour:** hospitals that pad bills or admit patients unnecessarily raise claim costs for everyone.
- **Ownership and conflicts:** who actually runs the place?

## What to verify

1. **Registration and licences:** the hospital's registration with the relevant authority, and any required licences.
2. **Business details:** the legal entity, its address and tax registrations.
3. **Facilities:** wards, beds, ICU, operation theatre, pharmacy and diagnostics, as declared.
4. **Staff:** doctors and nursing staff present, with registrations where required.
5. **Location:** the premises match the declared address, with live location recorded.
6. **Rate card and billing process:** how bills are raised and who approves them.
7. **Past complaints or claim patterns** where available to you.

## Why video helps

A physical audit remains the gold standard for some decisions, but it is slow and costly across many cities. A recorded live video tour lets a team see the reception, wards and equipment within a day, with location and time saved. It is also easy to repeat at intervals and to compare with the declared details.
