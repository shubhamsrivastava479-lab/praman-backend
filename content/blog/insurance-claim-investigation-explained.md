Every insurer investigates a share of its claims before paying them. For the honest claimant this can feel like delay; for the insurer it is how genuine claims are paid quickly while staged or inflated ones are stopped. A good investigation is fast, fair and well recorded.

## Why claims are investigated

- **Hidden facts at proposal:** illness, habits or existing policies not disclosed.
- **Staged or exaggerated events:** a hospitalisation that did not happen as described, or a motor accident that was arranged.
- **Document problems:** altered bills, reports or certificates.
- **Identity problems:** the claimant or the insured person is not who the papers say.

## What investigators check

### Health claims
- The patient's identity and presence in the hospital.
- Admission, treatment and discharge records against the bills.
- Past history of the illness and earlier treatment.

### Life and death claims
- Cause and place of death, and the documents that record it.
- The family's statement, the nominee's identity and relationship.
- Health history before the policy and income declared at the time.

### Motor claims
- The vehicle, its RC and the driver's licence.
- How, where and when the accident happened.
- Damage photos against the story.

## Common red flags

- Claims soon after the policy started.
- Statements that change between people or between visits.
- Hospital or address that cannot be located.
- Several policies bought shortly before the event.
