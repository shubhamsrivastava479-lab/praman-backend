Gold and vehicle loans are quick by design. The collateral gives lenders comfort, so decisions are made fast. But speed attracts fraud: gold that is not gold, vehicles that are already pledged, and borrowers who are not the owners. A standard set of quick checks keeps the loan fast and safe.

## Why it matters

A defaulted loan is only recoverable if the collateral is real, valuable and legally yours to take. Fraud in collateral often shows up only at recovery, when it is too late.

## Gold loan checks

1. **Identity:** photo ID matched with the borrower's face.
2. **Ownership:** does the borrower own the gold, or is it borrowed or stolen?
3. **Purity and weight:** tested in front of the borrower with proper equipment.
4. **Photos and record** of the items with the borrower in view.
5. **Source of funds and purpose** for larger amounts.
6. **Repeat borrowers:** unusual patterns of many loans.

## Vehicle loan checks

1. **Identity and address** of the borrower.
2. **RC details:** owner name, chassis and engine numbers, registration date.
3. **Hypothecation:** whether another lender already has a claim.
4. **Insurance** validity.
5. **Pending e-challans** and legal notices.
6. **Physical inspection** or live video of the vehicle.
7. **Income and repayment ability.**

## Red flags

- A borrower in a rush who avoids questions
- Name on the RC differs from the borrower's
- Gold with unusual weight or marks
- Several loans taken on the same day across branches

## Document the collateral

Store dated photos and a short video of the gold or vehicle with the borrower in view, along with the checks done. If the loan ever goes bad, this record supports recovery and any legal process.

Honest borrowers benefit too: a clear, fast process means they get money sooner and are not treated as suspects.
