When you buy a phone, a laptop, furniture or a vehicle online, you see photos. Photos are easy to copy. A scammer can take pictures from another listing and use them to take your advance. A short live video of the item costs the seller a minute and gives you real confidence.

## Why it matters

Many online fraud cases start with a listing for an item the seller does not own. Once the advance is paid, the seller disappears. A live video makes it much harder for a fake seller to continue.

## Why photos fall short

- They can be taken from another listing or website
- They can be edited
- They do not show the item's actual condition
- They do not prove the seller is present with it

## What a good video shows

1. **The item from several angles** including damage or wear.
2. **The serial or IMEI number,** chassis number or other identifier.
3. **Switching it on or using it,** where relevant.
4. **A live proof,** for example a note with today's date and your name.
5. **The seller on camera,** holding or standing next to the item.
6. **The surroundings,** so you know where it is.

## How to ask politely

- Say you want to be careful with the payment
- Offer a time that suits the seller
- Keep it short, a couple of minutes
- Thank them, since many genuine sellers will agree readily

## Combine with other checks

- Match the name of the payee with the seller
- Meet in a public place for local deals
- Pay only through safe methods and after verifying
- Keep chats and receipts

## If the seller says no

Do not pay. Genuine sellers understand. Excuses about being busy, being away or having someone else hold the item are common signs of a scam.
