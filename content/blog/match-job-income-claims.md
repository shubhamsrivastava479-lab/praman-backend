A good job and a stable income are often the first things a family looks at in a match. They are also the facts most easily exaggerated. A pleasant profile can hide a lost job, a small salary or a business that barely runs. Checking calmly and with consent protects both families.

## Why it matters

Marriage is a long commitment, often with shared loans, housing and plans for children. Learning about a hidden debt or an unreal job after the wedding is far harder to fix than learning it before the engagement.

## What to check

1. **The employer:** company name, role and how long the person has worked there.
2. **The salary:** recent pay slips and matching credits in bank statements.
3. **The business:** for self-employed people, registration, premises, customers and tax filings.
4. **Education and past jobs:** degrees and previous employers that match the story.
5. **Debts and obligations:** loans, dependants and family support the person must pay.
6. **Consistency:** do the job title, city and salary match what family and friends say?

## How to do it respectfully

- Ask both sides for the same information
- Explain it as a normal step, not suspicion
- Let the person share documents themselves, and hide numbers not needed such as full account numbers
- Verify with the employer using an official number you find yourself

## Warning signs

- A job title that sounds grand but the company cannot be found
- Pay slips with errors or no matching bank credits
- Reluctance to be on a live video call or to show documents
- Stories that change between meetings

## Beyond the paperwork

Numbers on paper are only part of the picture. Talk about work, colleagues and daily routine on a live call. Honest people usually answer easily and in detail, and gaps or vague answers are worth a gentle follow-up before the families go further.
