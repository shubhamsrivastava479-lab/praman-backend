A marriage joins two families, and the decision is often made on what one side tells the other, a profile on a website, or a word from a relative. Most matches are exactly who they say they are. But profiles with an inflated job, a hidden previous marriage or a false identity are a real worry for many families. A respectful, consent-based check gives both sides confidence.

## Why families verify

- **Profiles are easy to fake:** photos, job titles and salaries on matrimony sites are self-declared.
- **Job and income claims** are the most common source of disappointment later.
- **Identity:** the person you meet should be the person on the documents.
- **Family details and address:** where they actually live and with whom.
- **Previous marriage or legal cases** are sometimes not disclosed.

## What to check

1. **Identity:** Aadhaar and PAN matched to the person with a face match.
2. **Address:** current home and native place, confirmed with live location.
3. **Job:** employer, designation and how long, with an ID card or offer letter shown; employment history checks can add more, with consent.
4. **Education:** degree and institution as claimed.
5. **Declarations:** previous marriage, children, and any court or police case.
6. **A recorded statement** in their own words.

## How to bring it up without offending anyone

Make it mutual. Suggest that **both** families do the same short check, so it is about trust, not suspicion. Keep it private: share the report only between the two families.
