Matrimonial sites bring families together, and they also attract people who build false profiles. Some want to take money. Some use it to meet people under a false story. Some hide that they are already married. A calm routine can protect your family without turning every match into a suspect.

## Why it matters

Emotions run high in a matrimonial search, and the person on the other side may act quickly to build trust. Once money has been sent or private details shared, the damage is hard to undo.

## Signs of a fake profile

- A very attractive photo that looks like a model or stock picture
- A job or income that sounds too good for the age
- Reluctance to meet on video or to speak on the phone
- Overseas posting with a story about emergencies and money
- Moves quickly to private chat and talks of love or marriage within days
- Stories that change between conversations
- Requests for gifts, a loan or help with 'customs' or 'visa' costs

## What to do

1. **Reverse search the photos** to see if they appear elsewhere.
2. **Ask for a live video call** and ask simple questions about family and work.
3. **Verify identity and job** with consent, using documents and the employer's official contact.
4. **Meet through family,** with relatives present, before going further.
5. **Talk to people who know them,** such as relatives, friends and colleagues.
6. **Never send money** or share OTPs before you are sure.
7. **Report the profile** to the site and the cyber helpline if you are targeted.

## Protect yourself

- Share contact and address details gradually
- Keep chats and screenshots
- Involve a trusted elder early

## Involve family early

A trusted elder who joins the calls and asks questions often notices what excited young people miss. Taking time is not rude. Genuine matches will understand a family wanting to verify before committing.
