A delivery partner carries your customer's parcel to their door. Sometimes it is a phone, a gift or cash on delivery. Sometimes the partner enters a building or a home. Your brand travels with them. Good verification at onboarding protects customers, partners and the business.

## What can go wrong

- **Identity swaps:** the verified person hands the account to someone else.
- **Fake documents:** an edited licence or ID.
- **Cash and goods theft:** parcels or cash that never reach the customer.
- **Vehicle misuse:** a vehicle that is not registered to the partner or has pending fines.
- **Payout fraud:** payments to an account that is not the partner's.
- **Safety incidents** involving customers.

## The checks that matter

1. **Identity documents:** Aadhaar or PAN, matched with a face match and liveness check.
2. **Driving licence:** active, and covering the type of vehicle used.
3. **Vehicle details:** registration, owner and whether any fines are pending, for partners who use their own vehicle.
4. **Mobile number:** linked to the person.
5. **Bank account:** the holder name matches the partner before the first payout.
6. **Address:** a reachable home address with live location on the call.
7. **Court record check:** for higher-trust roles, with consent.
8. **Declarations and consent** recorded on the call.

## Making onboarding fast

- Send the link right after sign-up, so the partner does not wait for an appointment.
- Let instant checks run while the call takes place.
- Use a human reviewer only for flagged cases, such as a face mismatch or inconsistent answers.
- Offer the call in the partner's language.

## Do not stop at day one

Face re-checks at random or after a complaint help confirm that the person delivering is still the person you verified. Re-verify when documents expire or after a long gap.
