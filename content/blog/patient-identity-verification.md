When a patient is admitted, the pressure is on care, not paperwork. But getting identity right at that moment saves trouble later: wrong records, billing disputes, and in some cases insurance used by someone who is not the policyholder.

## Why it matters

- **Safety:** the right patient gets the right treatment and records.
- **Billing:** the claim is raised for the right person.
- **Insurance:** cashless approvals depend on the person matching the policy.
- **Dispute defence:** a clear record helps insurers, TPAs and hospitals if a claim is questioned.

## Risks hospitals and TPAs see

- Another person using a relative's or a friend's policy
- A patient using a different name
- Policy details that do not match the admitted patient
- Pre-existing conditions hidden in the admission history
- Repeat admissions across hospitals with different details

## A simple admission routine

1. **Ask for a photo ID** of the patient, or of the attendant if the patient is unwell.
2. **Compare the name, age and gender** with the insurance card and policy.
3. **Take a photo** of the patient at admission.
4. **Record the attendant's details** and relationship.
5. **Note the admission reason** and history in the patient's own words where possible.
6. **Flag differences** to the TPA early rather than at discharge.

## Handling emergencies

In emergencies, treat first and complete verification as soon as the patient is stable. Do not delay care for paperwork. Capture what you can and finish the rest later.

## Privacy matters

Patient information is sensitive. Collect only what is needed, store it securely, limit who can view it and explain why it is collected. Photos and ID copies should be used for admission and claim checks only, and deleted or archived as your policy and the law require.
