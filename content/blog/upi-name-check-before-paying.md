UPI made payments instant. The same speed that makes it convenient also makes mistakes and scams hard to undo. A few seconds spent on the name shown at payment is one of the simplest protections you have.

## Why the name matters

When you enter a UPI ID or scan a QR code, the payment app shows the name registered with the account before you confirm. That name tells you who will actually receive the money. Fraudsters rely on people not reading it.

## How scams use hurry

- **The advance payment:** a stranger selling an item asks for "token money" to hold it, then disappears.
- **The wrong person:** the QR code or UPI ID you were sent belongs to someone else.
- **The refund trick:** someone says they sent money by mistake and asks you to "return" it, or tells you to scan a code to "receive" money.
- **The changed account:** a vendor's payment details are changed by an email or message you cannot confirm.
- **The fake business:** a polished page with an account in a different name.

## A simple pre-payment routine

1. **Read the name** shown on screen before you press pay.
2. **Compare it with who you expect:** the seller, the shop or the vendor's legal name.
3. **Be cautious if it is a personal name** for a business, or a different name from the invoice.
4. **Never scan a code to receive money.** You only scan to pay.
5. **Send a small test amount** for a new payee if the deal is large and the person is genuine, then confirm receipt.
6. **Confirm changes by phone** on a number you already have.
7. **Keep screenshots and references** of the payment.

## For businesses

- Make vendors share their bank account details at onboarding and verify that the holder name matches the registered business.
- Reconfirm before changing any payment detail.
- Pay to accounts in the business's name, not an individual's, unless the vendor is a proprietor.
- Reconcile payments regularly.
