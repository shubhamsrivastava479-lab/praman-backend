Gig platforms live on speed. Every day a new partner waits for onboarding is a lost order. But the people they onboard enter customers' homes, carry their parcels and receive payouts. Verification that is too slow loses partners. Verification that is too light loses trust.

## The risks a platform actually faces

- **Account sharing:** one verified person, several people riding.
- **Identity swaps:** a verified account handed to someone else.
- **Payout fraud:** payments sent to an account that is not the partner's.
- **Document fraud:** a licence or ID that is not real.
- **Safety incidents** that damage the brand.

## What a practical onboarding check covers

1. **Identity documents:** PAN or Aadhaar and, for drivers, a driving licence with valid vehicle classes.
2. **Face match and liveness:** the person on the call matches the ID photo and is really present.
3. **Mobile number:** the number is linked to the person.
4. **Bank account:** the account holder name matches the partner before the first payout.
5. **Vehicle details** where the partner uses their own vehicle.
6. **Court record check** for roles of higher trust, such as home services.
7. **Consent and declarations** recorded on the call.

## Keeping it fast

- Do the check on the **partner's own phone** from a link, so there is no office visit.
- Run **instant checks in parallel** with the call, so there is no separate waiting step.
- Use **Excel upload or API** to feed new sign-ups straight into verification.
- Reserve a human review for cases that raise a flag.

## Re-verification matters

Verification at sign-up does not stop identity swaps a month later. A quick repeat face match, triggered at random or after a complaint, helps confirm the person working the account is the person you verified.
