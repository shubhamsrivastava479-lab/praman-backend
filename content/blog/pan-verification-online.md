The PAN is the most commonly collected ID in Indian finance, hiring and business onboarding. A quick online check tells you whether it is valid. It does not tell you everything, and treating it as full proof of identity is a common mistake.

## What a PAN check confirms

- The number follows the correct format and is a real, issued PAN
- Depending on the source, the holder's name matches the name you have
- In some cases, the status of the PAN, such as whether it is active
- A PAN's linked type, such as an individual or a company, from the fourth character

## What it does not confirm

- **That the person is the holder.** Someone can quote a relative's PAN.
- **Where they live or work.**
- **Their income or tax compliance.**
- **That the photo or card they show is genuine.**
- **That other details, like date of birth, are theirs,** unless those are returned and compared.

## How to use it well

1. **Ask for the PAN and the name as on the card.**
2. **Verify it** against an authorised source, with the person's consent.
3. **Compare the name** with the application and with other documents.
4. **Match the person's face** with the photo on the ID, live.
5. **Cross-check** with bank account name, address and phone where possible.
6. **Record the check** with date and result.

## Common problems

- Spelling or order differences in names
- Old PANs with outdated details
- A PAN belonging to a business used by an individual, or the reverse
- A card photographed from someone else's phone

## Privacy and consent

A PAN is sensitive personal data. Tell the person why you need it, collect it only for that purpose, store it securely and do not share it without need. Mask it in reports shown to people who do not need the full number.
