Health insurance works because many honest people share the cost of a few large claims. Fraud pushes premiums up for everyone. Insurers and TPAs therefore look carefully at some claims. Understanding the common types helps genuine customers see why questions are asked, and helps claims teams know what to look for.

## Common types of health claim fraud

**1. Non-disclosure of a pre-existing condition.** A known illness is left out of the proposal form, then claimed soon after the policy starts.

**2. Inflated bills.** Real treatment, but with charges padded, extra items added or bills altered.

**3. Fake or "paper" hospitalisation.** The patient was not actually admitted, or the stay was for a day-care procedure presented as a longer admission.

**4. Unnecessary treatment.** Admission or procedures that were not medically needed, sometimes arranged with a hospital.

**5. Identity misuse.** Another person is treated under the insured person's name.

**6. Duplicate or multiple claims.** The same bills submitted to more than one insurer.

**7. Forged or altered documents.** Discharge summaries, test reports or receipts that have been edited.

## Red flags investigators look for

- Claims soon after the policy begins
- Admission or discharge timings that do not match the story
- A hospital that cannot be located or is not as described
- Statements from the patient, family and hospital that do not agree
- Bills that do not match the treatment

## How investigations work

An investigator checks the patient's identity, the hospital, the treatment history and the documents, and asks the patient or family for their account. In a fair process, the claimant gets a chance to explain anything that looks unusual.
