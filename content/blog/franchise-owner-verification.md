A franchise is a long relationship and a large investment. Whether you are the brand granting a franchise or the person buying one, a mistake is expensive. Verifying the other side before you sign is the best protection.

## Why it matters

Franchisors can be hurt by weak or dishonest partners who damage the brand. Franchisees can lose their savings to brands that overpromise. Both sides benefit from honest checks.

## If you are the franchisor

1. **Identity:** the owner's ID with a live face match.
2. **Business and background:** experience, other ventures and past disputes.
3. **Financial strength:** ability to invest and survive the early months.
4. **Premises:** location, ownership or lease and suitability.
5. **Source of funds:** where the investment comes from.
6. **References:** past employers, partners and customers.
7. **Intent:** will they run the outlet themselves or leave it to others?

## If you are the franchisee

1. **The brand:** registration, trademark and how long it has operated.
2. **Existing outlets:** visit several and speak to owners without the franchisor present.
3. **Financial claims:** ask for real figures and how they were calculated.
4. **Fees and costs:** all fees, royalties and mandatory purchases.
5. **Legal history:** disputes or complaints against the brand.
6. **The agreement:** read it with a lawyer, especially about exit, territory and renewal.

## Red flags

- Guarantees of high returns with no data
- Pressure to pay a large fee quickly
- No existing franchisees you can speak to
- Reluctance to share the agreement in advance
- Unclear ownership of the brand

## Review regularly

Verification does not end at signing. Review sales, audits and customer feedback regularly, and revisit the checks if ownership, location or management changes. A clear, fair review process keeps the brand safe and the partnership healthy.
