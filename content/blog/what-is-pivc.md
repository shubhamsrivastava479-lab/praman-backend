Before a life or health policy is issued, many insurers call the customer to check that the sale was done properly. This is the pre-issuance verification call, or PIVC. It protects the customer from mis-selling and the insurer from complaints, cancellations and claims built on wrong information.

## Why PIVC matters

- **Mis-selling:** customers are sometimes told a policy is a fixed deposit, or promised returns that the product does not offer.
- **Wrong proposal details:** health, habits or income filled in by someone else.
- **Wrong person:** the person who signed is not the person who will be insured.
- **Early cancellations and complaints** cost the insurer and hurt trust.

## What a PIVC usually covers

1. **Identity** of the proposer and the person insured.
2. **Product:** the plan name, premium, payment term and policy term.
3. **Benefits and charges** in simple words.
4. **Promises:** was anything promised that is not in the policy, such as a bonus, loan or guaranteed return?
5. **Health and lifestyle:** confirming what was declared.
6. **Income and occupation**, where relevant to the cover.
7. **Consent** to go ahead.

## Phone PIVC versus video PIVC

A phone call confirms answers but cannot show who is answering. A **video PIVC** adds a face match to the ID, a liveness check, a recorded consent and a visual check of documents. It is much harder for an agent or relative to answer on the customer's behalf.
