Placement agencies make hiring quick. They find a cook, a nanny or a caregiver within days. But when a stranger will be in your home, relying on 'the agency has verified' is not enough. A few of your own checks cost little and add a lot of safety.

## Why it matters

Quality differs widely between agencies. Some do careful checks, others only collect an ID copy. If something goes wrong, the contract may leave you with little recourse.

## Questions to ask the agency

1. **How do you verify staff?** Ask for specifics, not a general answer.
2. **What documents do you hold?** ID, address proof, photo, police verification, references.
3. **Do you check previous employers?**
4. **What is the replacement policy?**
5. **What are your fees and terms for early exit?**
6. **Who is responsible if there is theft or harm?**
7. **Can I speak to a past client?**

## What to check yourself

- **Identity:** see the original photo ID and match the face with the person.
- **Address:** the current and permanent address, with a phone number of a family member.
- **References:** call at least one previous employer.
- **Police verification** as per local rules.
- **Health:** relevant fitness for the role.
- **Trial period:** a few weeks with clear tasks.

## Red flags

- The agency discourages you from checking or speaking to the worker directly
- Staff arrive with no documents
- Very high upfront fees with no written terms
- The worker's story differs from the agency's

## Keep records

Keep a copy of the agreement, the worker's details and the agency's contact. Share the worker's name and photo with a family member.

## Starting well

In the first weeks, be present when possible, set clear tasks and check in with the worker kindly. Good verification and a respectful start usually lead to a long, trusting relationship that is good for both sides.
