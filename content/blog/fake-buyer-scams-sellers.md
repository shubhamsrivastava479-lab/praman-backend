Selling a phone, furniture or a vehicle online should be simple. But fake buyers target sellers because sellers are in a hurry and want the deal done. A few simple habits take away most of the risk.

## Why it matters

Fake buyers do not need a hacked account. They need you to click, scan or share something. Once money leaves your account or a code is shared, getting it back is hard.

## Common tricks

- **Scan to receive:** a QR code or payment request that actually debits you.
- **Wrong amount sent:** the buyer 'accidentally' sends too much and asks for a refund while the first payment is fake or reversed.
- **Fake screenshots:** a payment screenshot with no money in your account.
- **Advance courier trick:** the buyer asks you to pay a small 'courier fee' first.
- **Army or official ID:** the buyer claims a posting and asks for urgent action.
- **Link to a lookalike site:** you are asked to enter card or bank details to 'receive' the payment.

## A safe routine

1. **Never scan or approve anything to receive money.**
2. **Check your own bank or UPI app,** not a screenshot, before handing over the item.
3. **Meet in a public place** for local sales, or use the platform's own protected payment.
4. **Do not share OTPs, PINs or card details,** ever.
5. **Check the buyer's name** against the payment name.
6. **Take a video call** with the buyer for high-value items.
7. **Keep chats and receipts** in case you need to report.

## Warning signs

- Pressure to move off the platform to another app
- Buyer unwilling to meet or speak live
- Payment arrives from a different name
- Urgency with excuses about travel or posting
