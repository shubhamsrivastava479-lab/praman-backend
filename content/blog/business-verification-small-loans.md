Small-business loans are built on trust in a business that often has limited records. That is why lenders look beyond documents. The goal is simple: does this business exist, is the applicant who they say, and does the scale match what they claim?

## Why it matters

Loans that go wrong usually fail at the first step. The shop is closed, the turnover was exaggerated, or the applicant is a front for someone else. A short, well-structured verification at the start costs far less than recovery later.

## What to verify

1. **The applicant:** identity, address and a live face match with the ID.
2. **The business exists:** registration or licence details, shop or office address, signboard.
3. **Ownership and role:** is the applicant the owner or a partner, and who else is involved?
4. **Premises:** is it owned or rented, and does the location match the application?
5. **Activity:** stock, customers, equipment, staff and daily operations you can actually see.
6. **Turnover and banking:** do bank statements and tax filings match what you see?
7. **Existing obligations:** other loans, dues and pending cases.

## A simple field-visit alternative

- Ask for a live video walk-through of the premises
- Match the location with the stated address
- Ask the owner simple questions about suppliers, customers and usual orders
- Compare answers with the documents

## Red flags

- The person on the call does not know basic details of the business
- Stock or activity is much smaller than the turnover claimed
- The address is a residential or shared space with no business signs
- Documents have mismatched names or dates

## Keep it proportionate

A small loan does not need the same depth as a large one. Set a basic standard for every applicant, and add extra checks, such as a second video visit or supplier calls, only when the amount is higher or something looks inconsistent. This keeps good borrowers moving and your team focused on real risk.
