Income decides how much a person can borrow and repay. It is also the number applicants most often inflate. A lender that checks it well avoids bad loans and still approves honest borrowers quickly.

## Why it matters

A loan based on a made-up salary starts with a problem. Most defaults in the early months trace back to overstated income or hidden obligations. A short, consistent income check is cheaper than collections.

## What to check

1. **Salary slips:** correct employer name, dates, deductions and arithmetic.
2. **Bank statements:** regular credits matching the slips, with no unusual cash deposits just before applying.
3. **Employment history:** how long the person has worked and where, supported by EPFO contributions where available.
4. **Name match:** the person's name, the account holder's name and the employer's records should align.
5. **Employer contact:** confirm through a number you find from official sources.
6. **Existing loans:** EMIs already being paid and how they affect repayment.
7. **Self-employed income:** turnover in bank statements, tax filings, GST and a view of the business.

## Common problems

- Edited salary slips or letterheads
- Credits from the same person or a related business
- Short employment history with a big salary claim
- Salary credited in cash

## A simple approach

Collect the documents, run basic checks on all, add deeper checks only for higher amounts, and speak to the applicant on a video call to confirm what the documents say.

## Keep the process fair

Explain to applicants which documents are needed and why, accept reasonable alternatives for people with irregular pay, and give a clear way to correct errors. Fair, consistent checks reduce disputes and speed up genuine approvals.

## Small habits that help

- Check that all pages of a bank statement are present and in sequence
- Compare the PDF with the bank's own download where possible
- Note down which documents you checked and when
