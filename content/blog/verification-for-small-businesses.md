Large companies have compliance teams and long onboarding forms. Small businesses usually have an owner, a few staff and trust. Trust is valuable, but it works best when it is supported by a simple process. The goal is not to suspect everyone. It is to know who you are dealing with before it becomes expensive.

## Where small businesses lose money

- A new employee handling cash, stock or accounts who turns out to have a history you never checked
- A supplier who takes an advance and vanishes
- A customer on credit whose business does not really exist
- A distributor or dealer who misuses your brand
- A payment sent to a bank account that was quietly changed

## A simple routine in four parts

**1. People who handle money or goods.** Before they start, confirm identity, address and past jobs. Call a previous employer yourself. For roles of high trust, add a court record check with consent.

**2. New vendors and suppliers.** Check the business registration and GST details, compare the legal name with the bank account name, and ask for a live look at the premises on video.

**3. Customers on credit.** Before you extend credit, confirm the business is real and who the owner is. Start with small limits and increase as they pay.

**4. Partners, dealers and franchisees.** Verify the owner's identity and the outlet, and repeat the check each year.

## Habits that matter more than tools

- **Write down your rule:** who gets checked, and what you check.
- **Always take consent** and explain why.
- **Never change a bank account** on an email or a message alone. Confirm by calling a known number.
- **Keep records** in one place, not scattered across phones.
- **Treat mismatches as questions**, not accusations.

## Keep it light

Not every check needs a visit. A short video call can confirm who a person is, where they work and what they declare. Save field visits for cases where something does not add up.
