Health insurance fraud is not only about patients. Sometimes a hospital bills for admissions that never happened, or for patients who do not exist. These 'ghost patients' and fake admissions cost insurers, and eventually every policyholder, a lot of money.

## Why it matters

Fake claims drive up premiums and reduce trust between insurers and hospitals. Catching them early protects honest hospitals and members.

## How it happens

- **Ghost patient:** a person who was never admitted, or who does not exist.
- **Fake admission:** someone admitted for a day for a claim that did not need hospital care.
- **Inflated treatment:** extra tests or stays billed but not done.
- **Identity misuse:** a policyholder's details used by another person.
- **Repeat claims:** the same person admitted often at the same hospital.

## Red flags

- Admission and discharge at odd hours or within very short stays
- Documents with the same wording and format across many patients
- Patient not reachable, or does not know about the treatment
- Photo ID that does not match the person
- Treatment inconsistent with the diagnosis
- Many claims from the same address, phone or doctor

## How TPAs check

1. **Verify the patient's identity** with ID and a live face check.
2. **Call or video the patient** to confirm admission, treatment and dates.
3. **Check hospital records,** registers and bills for consistency.
4. **Compare with past claims** for patterns.
5. **Audit the hospital** for staff, beds and equipment.
6. **Review in detail** the high-risk cases only.

## For genuine patients

Keep your discharge summary, bills, reports and prescriptions, and respond to the TPA's calls so your claim is not delayed.

## Fairness to hospitals

Most hospitals are honest. Share findings with them, give them a chance to explain, and focus action on clear evidence. A transparent, consistent process builds trust between hospitals, TPAs and insurers.
