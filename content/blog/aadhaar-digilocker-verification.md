For years, "send me a copy of your Aadhaar" has meant a photocopy or a WhatsApp photo, which is then stored on someone's phone, forwarded to colleagues and kept forever. DigiLocker offers a better way: the person shares their own document, with their own consent, from the source.

## What DigiLocker is

DigiLocker is a government platform where people can keep and share digital versions of official documents. According to the platform's own figures, it had 72.43 crore users as of August 2026. Documents issued by government departments can be fetched into a person's account and shared with others on request.

## How the consent-based flow works

1. A business asks the person to share a document, such as their Aadhaar details.
2. The person is taken to DigiLocker and logs in with their own mobile number and OTP.
3. The person **sees what is being requested** and chooses to allow it.
4. The requested details come directly from the source and are passed to the business.

The key difference from a photocopy is **consent and provenance**: the person decides, and the data comes from the issuing system, not from a scanned image.

## Why it is safer than photocopies

- A photocopy can be edited. A document fetched from the source is much harder to forge.
- The person controls what is shared and can see the request.
- Businesses can avoid holding full Aadhaar copies in phones and inboxes.
- There is a clearer record of consent.

## Using it responsibly

- **Ask only for what you need.** Do not collect an Aadhaar number if a different ID will do.
- **Prefer masked numbers.** Do not store the full number unless the law allows it and you have a real need.
- **Explain why** you are asking and how long you will keep the data.
- **Keep it secure:** limit who can see it, and delete it when the purpose is over.
- Check the current Aadhaar and data protection rules with your compliance team.
