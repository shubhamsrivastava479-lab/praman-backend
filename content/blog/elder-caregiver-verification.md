When parents grow old, family members often cannot be there all day. A caregiver or attendant steps in to help with medicines, meals, mobility and company. It is a role built on trust, and it is also one where a person may be vulnerable and unable to complain. That is why checking before you hire matters.

## Why this role needs extra care

- Elderly people may have memory problems, poor hearing or difficulty speaking up.
- The caregiver often has access to medicines, cash, jewellery and documents.
- Some people claim medical skills they do not have.
- Families living in another city cannot easily watch what happens day to day.

## Checks before you hire

1. **Identity:** ID matched to the person, not only a photocopy.
2. **Home address and family contact:** where they live and who can be reached in an emergency.
3. **Experience:** what care they have given, for how long and for whom.
4. **References:** call the previous family yourself and ask specific questions about reliability and conduct.
5. **Skills and certifications:** if they claim nursing skills, ask what training they have and test with a practical conversation.
6. **Health:** basic honesty about any illness that could be passed on.
7. **Court record check** with consent, for roles of high trust.
8. **A recorded statement** in their own words.

## Matching the person to the parent

Experience in one type of care does not fit all. A caregiver for a person with dementia needs different skills from one for a person recovering from surgery. Ask how they would handle your parent's needs in particular.

## After they start

- Begin with a trial period and check in often.
- Ask your parent, gently, how they feel with the caregiver.
- Keep medicines and valuables secure and keep a record of cash handled.
- Speak with neighbours or visiting relatives about what they notice.
- Have a backup plan if the caregiver is absent.
