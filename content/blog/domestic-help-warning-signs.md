Hiring a cook, a maid, a driver or a caregiver is a decision based on trust. Most people who work in homes are honest and hardworking. But a few warning signs, noticed early, can help you avoid a bad hire. Be alert and fair at the same time.

## Why it matters

Domestic workers have access to your home, belongings and family. A mistake can mean theft, harm or an unsafe situation for children or elders. A few minutes spent noticing the signs at the start is worth it.

## Warning signs in documents

- No ID, or an ID that looks edited or does not match the person
- Different names on different papers
- No local address, or an address that cannot be confirmed
- References who cannot be reached or who sound rehearsed

## Warning signs in behaviour

- Reluctance to share contact details or a family member's number
- A story about previous jobs that keeps changing
- Asking for a large advance on the first day
- Unusual curiosity about valuables, schedules or when the house is empty
- Avoiding questions about why they left earlier jobs

## Warning signs with agencies

- Pressure to hire at once
- No written terms or receipts for fees
- The agency refuses to share the worker's documents
- Promises of 'complete verification' without details

## What to do

1. **Verify identity and address** before they start.
2. **Call references** yourself.
3. **Start with a trial period** and clear tasks.
4. **Share the worker's details** with a family member or neighbour.
5. **Keep valuables secure** without making it personal.
6. **Be respectful,** treat them fairly and pay on time.

Good treatment, clear terms and fair pay also reduce problems. A worker who feels respected is more likely to stay and to be honest.
