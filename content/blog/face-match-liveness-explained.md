Many verifications today start with a selfie. Behind that simple step are two separate checks: face match and liveness. They answer different questions, and understanding them helps you use them correctly.

## Face match

A face match compares two images, for example the photo on an ID card and a selfie taken now, and says how similar they are. It helps confirm that the person in front of you is the person on the document.

## Liveness

A liveness check asks whether a real person is present at this moment. The person may be asked to blink, turn their head or follow a prompt. This makes it harder to pass with a printed photo, a screen or a pre-recorded video.

## Why you need both

- Face match alone can be fooled by someone holding up another person's photo
- Liveness alone does not tell you whether the person is the one on the ID
- Together they link a live person to an identity document

## Where they help

- Customer onboarding and loan applications
- Hiring and worker verification
- Gig platform and delivery partner checks
- Remote policy and claim verification

## Limits to know

- Poor light, camera quality and angles can lower accuracy
- Twins, ageing and big changes in appearance can cause mismatches
- Deepfakes are getting better, so checks should be layered with other signals
- A mismatch is a reason to review, not proof of fraud

## Good practice

- Tell people why and how their face data is used
- Offer a fallback to a human reviewer
- Keep the data secure and delete it when not needed
- Review mismatches before taking action

## Choosing a service

Ask how results are reviewed, what happens on a mismatch, how long face data is kept and whether there is a way to retry. A good process helps genuine people pass easily while still catching misuse.
