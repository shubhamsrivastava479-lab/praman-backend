When you buy a used vehicle, the chassis and engine numbers are its fingerprints. They should match the registration certificate (RC) exactly. A mismatch can mean a replaced engine, a stolen vehicle, a cloned registration or a document problem that will cause trouble later.

## Why it matters

A vehicle with wrong or tampered numbers can be impounded, may not be transferable and can leave you with no insurance claim. Checking takes minutes, and the cost of missing it can be the full price of the vehicle.

## What the numbers are

- **Chassis number:** a unique code stamped on the vehicle's frame.
- **Engine number:** a code on the engine block.
- **Both are printed on the RC** along with the registration number and the owner's name.

## How to match them

1. **Get the RC or its details** and note both numbers.
2. **Find the chassis number** on the vehicle, usually on the frame, near the dashboard or in the door area, and compare character by character.
3. **Find the engine number** on the engine block and compare.
4. **Look for tampering:** uneven stamping, scratches, welds or re-painted spots.
5. **Check the registration number plate** and compare with the RC.
6. **Look up the RC details** with the registration number and compare with what you see.
7. **Take photos** of both numbers with the vehicle in view.

## What a mismatch can mean

- Engine replaced without updating the RC
- A typing or entry error in the RC
- A cloned or forged RC
- A stolen vehicle given a new identity

## What to do

- Pause the deal
- Ask the seller for documents explaining the change
- Get a mechanic or the authority to inspect
- Complete transfer only after any correction
