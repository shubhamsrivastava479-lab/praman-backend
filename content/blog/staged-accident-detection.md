Most accident claims are honest. A small number are staged: planned to look like an accident, or made bigger than they were. They add to everyone's premiums and cost insurers heavily. Investigators look for patterns that cannot easily be hidden.

## Why it matters

Staged accidents involve people, vehicles and sometimes false medical or repair claims. Spotting them early protects honest customers from higher costs and stops repeat offenders.

## Patterns investigators look for

- A claim soon after a policy starts or a lapse ends
- Accidents at quiet locations with no independent witnesses
- The same people, vehicles or garages appearing in several claims
- Damage that does not match the described accident
- Injuries inconsistent with the impact
- Late reporting with no clear reason
- Statements that differ between the driver, owner and passengers
- A vehicle already damaged before the claimed date

## How investigators check

1. **Compare statements** from everyone involved, taken separately.
2. **Visit or video the scene** and compare it with the story.
3. **Inspect the vehicle** and match damage with the accident type.
4. **Check records:** RC, licence, past claims, challans and location data.
5. **Check hospitals and garages** for genuine bills and treatment.
6. **Look for links** between the people, vehicles and repair shops.

## What genuine claimants can do

- Report as soon as possible
- Take photos and videos of the scene and the damage
- Keep the FIR, estimates and bills
- Give an honest, consistent account

## Fair process

An investigation is not an accusation. Good insurers tell the claimant what is needed, listen to explanations and decide on evidence. Genuine claimants who cooperate usually see their claims settled with little delay.

Insurers that explain their steps and timelines reduce stress and complaints for everyone involved in a claim.
