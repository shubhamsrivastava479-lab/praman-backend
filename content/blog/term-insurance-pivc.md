Term insurance pays a large sum only if something goes wrong, which is why insurers pay close attention to it at the start. A pre-issuance verification call (PIVC) on a high cover goes beyond confirming a name. It confirms who is buying, whether the cover fits their life, and whether they have told the full truth.

## Why high covers need more care

- The sum assured is large compared with income
- Health and habits strongly affect the risk
- Early claims are costly and sensitive
- Policies may be bought by or for someone other than the insured

## The extra questions

1. **Identity and consent:** are you the person being insured, and did you apply yourself?
2. **Income and occupation:** what do you do and what do you earn?
3. **Reason for the cover:** who depends on you, and what loans or needs does it cover?
4. **Health disclosure:** any illness, surgery, treatment or medication, past or present?
5. **Habits:** smoking, alcohol or risky activities?
6. **Other policies:** existing insurance and claims?
7. **Nominee:** who is the nominee and what is the relationship?
8. **Understanding:** do you know the premium, term and what is not covered?

## Good practice on the call

- Use clear, simple language
- Let the customer answer in their own words
- Do not lead them to say 'no' to health questions
- Record the answers with date and time

## Signs to flag

- A sum assured far above visible income
- Reluctance to speak about health
- Someone else answering on the person's behalf
- Hesitation or conflicting answers

## Be kind and clear

Many customers find insurance language confusing. A good call explains terms in simple words, pauses for questions and confirms understanding. Clear answers protect the customer's family later, and protect the insurer from disputes.
