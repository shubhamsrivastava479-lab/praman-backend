Delivery apps, ride platforms and service marketplaces verify a partner when they join. But the verified person is not always the one doing the work. Account sharing, where someone else uses a registered partner's login, is a real problem for safety, insurance and customer trust.

## Why account sharing matters

- **Safety:** customers and goods are handled by someone the platform never checked.
- **Fraud:** blocked or ineligible people return through another person's account.
- **Insurance and liability:** an incident may involve an unverified person.
- **Fairness:** honest partners lose orders to rented accounts.
- **Compliance:** a platform may be required to know who is working.

## Signs of sharing

- Different faces or voices in customer feedback
- Logins from many devices or distant locations
- Sudden changes in speed, areas or behaviour
- Customer complaints about a person who does not match the profile

## How liveness and face match help

1. **Verify at onboarding:** ID plus a face match.
2. **Re-check at intervals** or when risk signals appear, with a short selfie or video.
3. **Match to the original face** and require a live prompt so photos or videos cannot be used.
4. **Check before high-risk tasks,** such as high-value deliveries or night shifts.
5. **Handle failures kindly:** give a way to retry or to contact support before any action.

## Good practice

- Explain why and how often
- Keep the step under a minute
- Protect the stored face data and delete what you no longer need
- Treat mismatches as questions first, not accusations

## A balanced approach

The aim is not to punish partners but to keep the platform safe for everyone. Combine light, occasional checks with clear communication, quick support for genuine problems and firm action only after a mismatch is confirmed by a person. Partners who work honestly usually welcome a system that protects their earnings from rented accounts.
