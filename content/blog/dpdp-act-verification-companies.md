India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 sets the rules for how organisations handle personal data. The DPDP Rules were notified on 13 November 2025, with obligations phased in over the following months. Verification, whether hiring checks, KYC, tenant checks or claim investigations, is all about personal data, so it sits right in the middle of this law.

*This is a general overview, not legal advice.*

## Why it matters for verification

Verification collects some of the most sensitive data a person has: ID numbers, photos, addresses, employment history and sometimes financial details. The Act's penalties are serious, up to ₹250 crore for a failure to take reasonable security safeguards.

## The key principles in practice

1. **Consent with notice:** tell the person what data you will collect, why, and how they can withdraw. Take their consent clearly before you start.
2. **Purpose limitation:** use the data only for the verification you described.
3. **Data minimisation:** collect only what the check needs. Do not keep extra ID copies "just in case".
4. **Security:** encryption, access control and logs of who saw what.
5. **Retention:** delete data when the purpose is done, unless a law requires you to keep it.
6. **Rights:** the person can ask what you hold and ask for correction or erasure.
7. **Processors:** if a vendor runs checks for you, you remain responsible. Choose vendors who can show their controls.

## Common gaps

- Consent taken on paper with no record of what was explained.
- Candidate documents shared over personal email or WhatsApp groups.
- No deletion policy, so old data stays forever.
- No log of who accessed a report.
