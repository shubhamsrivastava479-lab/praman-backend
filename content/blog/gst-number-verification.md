A GST number is one of the first documents a new vendor, dealer or seller gives you. It looks official, and it is easy to copy onto an invoice. Before you rely on it, verify it, and understand what it does and does not prove.

## What a GSTIN looks like

A Goods and Services Tax Identification Number (GSTIN) has **15 characters**:

- the first **2** are the state code
- the next **10** are the PAN of the business or person
- the **13th** is the number of registrations under the same PAN in that state
- the **14th** is usually the letter Z
- the **15th** is a check character

This structure helps you spot obviously wrong numbers. If the PAN inside does not match the vendor's PAN, ask why.

## What to check

1. **Does the number exist and is it active?** A cancelled or suspended registration is a warning.
2. **Does the legal name match?** Compare it with the vendor's invoice, bank account name and company papers.
3. **Does the address match?** Check the principal place of business against where the vendor says they operate.
4. **Is the PAN inside the GSTIN the same as the vendor's PAN?**
5. **What is the type of registration?** For example, regular or composition.
6. **Are there other GSTINs under the same PAN?** This shows other states where the business is registered.

## What a GST number does not prove

- That a **shop, factory or office actually exists** at that address
- That the business has the **capacity** it claims
- That the person you are speaking to is the **owner**
- That the business is **honest** or financially sound

## Add a site check and a bank check

Pair the GST check with a live look at the premises and a check that the bank account name matches the vendor. Many frauds use a real GSTIN of one business to impersonate it.
