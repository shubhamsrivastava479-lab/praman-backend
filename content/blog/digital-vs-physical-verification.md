For decades, verification meant a person visiting an address, meeting someone and writing a report. Today much of it can be done on a video call from the other person's phone. Both methods have a place. The question is which to use, and when.

## Side-by-side

| | Physical visit | Digital video verification |
|---|---|---|
| Time | Several days to schedule and travel | Often the same day |
| Cost | Travel, time, field staff | No travel |
| Reach | Hard in remote areas | Anywhere with a phone and data |
| Evidence | Photos and a written note | Recorded video, GPS, face match, timestamps |
| Risk of fake visit | Exists | Location and liveness are captured live |
| Person's convenience | Must be present at a fixed time | Joins when free, in their language |

## When digital verification works best

- High volumes: hiring, PIVC, lending, tenant checks.
- People spread across many cities or villages.
- When you need a recorded, auditable statement.
- When speed matters to the customer.

## When to keep physical visits

- The person cannot reach the place on camera, or there is no network.
- The case has strong red flags and needs on-ground checking with neighbours or records.
- Legal or contract terms require a physical inspection.

## The hybrid approach

Start digital. Let video verification clear the large majority quickly, and send a field investigator only to the cases that need it. You get speed and lower cost without losing depth.
