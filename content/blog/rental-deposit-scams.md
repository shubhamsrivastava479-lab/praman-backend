Finding a rented home is stressful, and scammers use that. Fake owners, brokers who are not what they say and flats that are not available all cause tenants to lose a deposit. A few checks before you pay can keep you safe.

## Why it matters

Deposits and advance rents can be many months of rent. Once paid to a fake owner, getting the money back is difficult. Even genuine cases can turn into disputes if the person who took the money was not authorised.

## How the scams work

- **Fake owner:** a person who has access to a flat, such as a caretaker or former tenant, poses as the owner.
- **Duplicate listing:** one flat shown to many people for a 'token'.
- **Copied photos and ads:** listings of flats that do not exist.
- **Urgent offer:** low rent if you pay immediately.
- **Remote deal:** the 'owner' lives abroad and asks you to pay first.

## What to check

1. **See the property in person** and confirm the address.
2. **Ask for ownership papers,** such as a sale deed or recent property tax or utility bill.
3. **Check the owner's ID** and match the name with the papers.
4. **Meet the owner** or the person with a written authority, and ask the society or neighbours.
5. **Use a written agreement** with the correct names, and register it where required.
6. **Pay by traceable methods** into an account in the owner's name, and keep receipts.
7. **Do not pay a large deposit** before checks and the agreement are done.

## Warning signs

- Rent far below nearby flats
- Refusal to meet or to show papers
- Pressure to pay today
- Payment requested to a personal account with a different name

## If you have been cheated

Report it quickly to the local police and the cybercrime portal, and keep chats, receipts and the listing. Share the details with the platform where you found the flat so that others are warned.
