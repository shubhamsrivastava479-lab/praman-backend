Most tenants are honest. But a bad tenancy can mean months of unpaid rent, damage and legal trouble, and some property is rented to people using false identities. Knowing the warning signs helps you ask the right questions early.

## The nine red flags

1. **Eager to move in immediately** and not interested in seeing the terms.
2. **Offers to pay several months in advance in cash** to avoid checks.
3. **Reluctance to share ID** or to let you keep a copy.
4. **Names that do not match** across ID, bank account and agreement.
5. **No verifiable job or business,** or an employer that cannot be reached.
6. **A story that changes** about who will live in the property or why they are moving.
7. **Refuses police verification** or tenant registration.
8. **No previous landlord reference,** or a reference who is a friend.
9. **Too many occupants** for the size of the home, or a commercial use that was not stated.

## How to check calmly

- Explain that you ask everyone the same questions
- Collect ID, a photo, employer details and references
- Speak to the employer and the previous landlord using numbers you find yourself
- Meet or video-call the main tenant and every adult occupant
- Write the agreement in the real names of the people who will live there

## What to keep on record

- Copies of ID, with consent
- The rent agreement and registration where required
- Police intimation or verification as per local rules
- Photos of the property's condition at move-in

## If you find a problem

Stay calm and professional. Ask for an explanation, request the missing document, and do not hand over keys until the checks you need are complete. If you suspect fraud, keep copies of what was shared and speak to the local police or a lawyer about next steps.
