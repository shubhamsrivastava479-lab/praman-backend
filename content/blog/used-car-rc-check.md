A used car or bike can be a great deal. It can also come with someone else's loan, unpaid challans, or a seller who is not actually the owner. Ten minutes of checking before you pay is far cheaper than months of trying to transfer a vehicle that is not cleanly yours.

## Why the check matters

- **The seller is not the owner.** Vehicles are sometimes sold by a relative, a broker or someone with no right to sell.
- **A loan is still running.** If the RC shows hypothecation to a bank, the transfer needs the bank's NOC.
- **Pending challans** can hold up the ownership transfer.
- **Mismatched numbers:** the chassis or engine number on the vehicle does not match the RC.
- **Lapsed insurance** leaves you exposed from the first day of driving.

## What to check, step by step

1. **RC details:** registration number, owner name, registration date, fuel and model. These come from the official vehicle registry.
2. **Chassis and engine number:** compare the RC with the numbers on the vehicle itself.
3. **Hypothecation:** is a bank or finance company still marked? If yes, ask for the loan closure letter and NOC.
4. **Insurance:** is the policy active, and in whose name?
5. **Challans:** any unpaid fines against the number.
6. **The seller:** their ID matched to the RC owner name, and their face matched to that ID.
7. **Paperwork for transfer:** the forms for change of ownership and, where needed, an NOC for another state.

## Common red flags

- The seller wants to sell "on power of attorney" without transferring the RC.
- The price is far below the market for no clear reason.
- The seller avoids a video call or keeps changing the meeting place.
- The owner's name on the RC is different and "will be sorted later".
