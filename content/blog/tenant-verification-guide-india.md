Renting out a flat looks simple: find a tenant, sign an agreement, hand over the keys. But the person living in your property for the next eleven months will have your address, your furniture and, in many cases, your neighbours' trust. A short verification before the agreement is the cheapest protection a landlord can buy.

## Why tenant verification matters

Most tenants are genuine. The trouble is that you cannot tell which ones are not from a phone call or a photocopy. Problems landlords commonly face include:

- **False identity:** the name on the agreement is not the person who moves in.
- **Unpaid rent and disappearing tenants:** no reliable permanent address to follow up.
- **Illegal use of the property:** sub-letting, commercial use or worse.
- **Disputes later:** with no record of what the tenant said at the start, it is your word against theirs.

## What a good tenant check covers

1. **Identity:** Aadhaar and PAN seen and matched to the person, not just collected as copies.
2. **Face match and liveness:** the person on the call is the person on the ID, and is really present.
3. **Current and permanent address:** where they live now, where their family lives, and since when.
4. **Work and income:** where they work and roughly what they earn, so the rent is realistic.
5. **Previous address and reason for moving:** a quick way to spot a story that does not add up.
6. **Declarations:** whether any police or court case has been filed against them.
7. **A record:** a video statement with date, time and location you can keep.

## Police verification and background checks are different

Police verification is about informing the police who lives in your property. In many cities, the police ask landlords to submit tenant details, often online. A background check is about **your** decision: is this person who they say they are, and does their story hold together? Do both. One does not replace the other.

## How to verify a tenant, step by step

1. Ask the tenant for their full name and WhatsApp number, and tell them you will do a short verification. Most genuine tenants are happy to do it.
2. Send the verification request and let the tenant take the call when they are free.
3. On the call, the tenant shows their ID cards, takes a face photo and liveness check, and answers questions about work, address and household.
4. Read the report. Look at mismatches: a name that differs from the ID, an address that does not match the live location, vague answers about work.
5. Keep the report with your rent agreement.
