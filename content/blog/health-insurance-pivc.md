In health insurance, what you say on the proposal and on the verification call decides whether a claim is paid years later. A pre-issuance verification call (PIVC) is designed to make sure that the customer understood the questions and answered honestly.

## Why disclosure matters

Insurers accept and price health policies based on what they are told. If an illness, surgery or medicine is left out, a claim that relates to it can be reduced or rejected even if the premium was paid for years. A clear conversation at the start prevents that pain.

## What the call usually covers

1. **Identity and consent:** the person confirms who they are and that they bought the policy.
2. **Members covered:** names, ages and relationships.
3. **Pre-existing conditions:** diabetes, blood pressure, heart conditions, thyroid, past surgeries and ongoing treatment.
4. **Medicines and tests:** regular medicines and recent investigations.
5. **Habits:** smoking and alcohol.
6. **Family history** where asked.
7. **Understanding of terms:** waiting periods, co-pay, room rent limits and exclusions.
8. **Premium and renewal.**

## Tips for customers

- Keep prescriptions and reports handy before the call
- Answer for every member covered, not only yourself
- Ask the caller to repeat or explain anything unclear
- Do not rely on an agent's assurance if it differs from the form

## Tips for insurers

- Ask one question at a time, in the customer's language
- Do not lead the customer to say 'no'
- Record the answers and check them against the proposal
- Flag unclear answers for follow-up before issuing

## After the call

Keep a copy of what you declared and the policy wording. If your health changes or you add members later, update the insurer in writing so that the record always matches the facts.
